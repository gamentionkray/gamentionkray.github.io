# Design System: Krishna Thorat Portfolio

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Forty-four worlds, one factual portfolio"**

The 44 existing named themes are the user-pinned visual directions. The confirmed experience preference is fully immersive; the implementation is code-led. This document records implemented decisions, not a user-approved screenshot or visual comp. “Forty-four worlds, one factual portfolio” is an implementation descriptor, not a separately approved brand metaphor.

Each world owns its whole page: opening composition, project browsing, lower career/toolkit/learning grammar, navigation and functional interaction. Shared profile text and small data-rendering helpers provide consistency of facts without imposing a shared hero or content flow. The isolated picker remains a stable way to travel between worlds.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Forty-four independent page compositions and theme-specific behaviours.
- Shared factual résumé content, portrait and downloadable PDF.
- Self-hosted Sans, Serif and Mono type families.
- Readable responsive layouts, native disclosures and an isolated screenshot picker.

Source of truth: `PRODUCT.md` holds durable product constraints; `.impeccable/theme-worlds-contract.md` holds the confirmed experience contract; `data/profile.json` holds facts; `data/themes.json` holds all route/palette/font/catalogue metadata. `scripts/worlds.mjs` owns each complete composition. `scripts/build.mjs` generates HTML and the browser data files. `assets/css/worlds.css`, `assets/js/worlds.js` and `assets/js/template-switcher.js` own visual rules, local world behaviours and picker implementation. Generated HTML and `assets/js/profile.js` / `themes.js` are outputs. This document and its sidecar are implementation records; update them when those maintained sources change.

## Colors

### Primary

Every named world has its own accent; the frontmatter records all 44 palette triples under route-prefixed paper, ink and accent names. In generated pages these bind to `--bg`, `--fg` and `--accent`. Do not apply Minimal’s green as a global identity. Range-driven light, signal, evening and material states live in the world CSS and JavaScript rather than creating a second palette registry here.

### Neutral

Line, wash and muted tones derive from the active foreground/background pair. Paper and ink are semantic roles and can be dark or light. Material-specific surfaces remain contextual to their world. Picker paper, ink, accent, muted, line, white and focus tokens are independent.

**The Picker Isolation Rule.** Page colours must not enter the picker’s Shadow DOM palette.

## Typography

**Display Font:** self-hosted Sans, Serif or Mono selected by the catalogue font field, with Arial/sans-serif, Georgia/serif and monospace fallbacks. **Body Font:** Sans by default; research paper, industrial and other world selectors establish their own reading type. **Label/Mono Font:** Mono for shell, technical output and selected metadata. The picker uses Arial independently. Font files are `assets/fonts/{sans,serif,mono}.otf` with local bold Sans and Serif faces; the current face rules do not declare `font-display`.

### Hierarchy

Frontmatter records baseline roles. Theme heading sizes and weights intentionally override them: poster lettering, paper title, app identity and book spine are different hierarchies. Paragraphs cap at 70ch and baseline introductions at 46ch. Phone body text becomes 16px. Preserve the world’s hierarchy rather than normalizing every title to the baseline display size.

## Layout

Baseline main caps at 1600px and uses the frontmatter main padding. Individual worlds replace that baseline with paper margins, desktop planes, an application rail, mosaic, orbital map, terrain, quest journal, scroll, contact sheet, record sleeve, bookcase or route map. World CSS narrows at 1050px and 700px; phone layouts use the frontmatter phone padding, collapse grids and adapt mechanism size. Desktop windows become a flow, paper navigation becomes wrapping links, the app retains bottom tabs, and the atlas maps become a readable stop list. Compact RPG and Dota first viewports keep quest and ability controls in view on a 390px phone.

**The Whole World Rule.** Opening, project browsing, lower records and navigation must all speak the selected world’s grammar.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth belongs to each world. Newspaper and scientific paper rely on rules; brutalist stickers use hard offset shadows; clay uses inset highlights and diffuse shadows; isometric buildings use skewed faces and drop shadows; origami layers folded sheets; desktops use window shadows. The sidecar records representative exact source values. The stable picker uses separate launcher and drawer shadows.

## Shapes

Baseline buttons and rules are square. Clay objects are rounded and soften further in their explicit state; isometric buildings use skewed walls, orbital controls use circles, film frames use print borders, terrain uses discrete rectangular cells, books use spine silhouettes, and desktop handles signal movable surfaces. Default portrait is a 3:4 crop; theme selectors set specific crops or grayscale. There is no global portrait grayscale rule.

## Components

### Buttons

Native controls use a 44px baseline minimum height; selected project buttons expose `aria-pressed` and invert foreground/paper. World materials may override their appearance. Page focus is a 3px accent outline offset 4px. The isolated picker uses its fixed focus colour at 3px offset; its filter controls and close button use their own source dimensions.

### Chips

Technology stacks are compact labels with underlined rules by default; themes restyle them as materials, records or inventory. Picker category buttons use `aria-pressed` and independent selected colours.

### Cards / Containers

Project facts are shared atoms; surrounding layout, article grammar, details, borders and depth are world-owned. Native `details` / `summary` provide accordion, quest, folded leaf, book and programme exploration. Interactive project readers render real project facts, not fabricated samples. Full project facts are also present in static HTML disclosures.

### Inputs / Fields

Native ranges control document scale, foil, drawing zoom, daylight or exploration depth. Terminal input runs a bounded local command vocabulary without eval or remote execution. Library search filters catalogue records and exposes empty states. Weathered notes save only on the current device; if storage is unavailable, the displayed status reports visit-only persistence.

### Navigation

Skip links and semantic anchors remain available. Theme navigation may be a rail, paper margin, folder row, taskbar, bottom app tabs, case tabs or project index. The normal Themes link leads to `themes.html`; JavaScript enhances it to open the picker. The modal focuses search, wraps keyboard focus, restores focus on close and locks background scroll. Category/search results and interactive world outputs use native status or live regions where implemented.

### World interactions

Window and sticker movement supports pointer dragging and arrow-key equivalents. Kanban has drag-and-drop and a labelled Move to select. Terrain and planet controls are native buttons; pixel exploration supports arena arrow keys, direction buttons and direct signal selection. Dota shortcuts ignore modifier keys, inputs and the theme picker. Optional chalk freehand drawing is pointer-only and carries no portfolio information.

LocalStorage is limited to picker selection (`portfolio-theme`), RPG explored quests (`kt-quests`) and field notes (`kt-field-note`). Terrain edits, game collection, card placement, window positions, drawings and cosmetic toggles last only in the current page. Contact download creates a local vCard. No notes, shell commands or exploration state are transmitted.

Reduced motion disables decorative CSS animation and transitions and changes smooth scroll to automatic; JavaScript-triggered scroll uses an instant path. No portfolio text requires animation or a wait to appear. With JavaScript disabled, the generated factual portfolio, résumé link, section navigation and native disclosures remain readable; enhanced games and local controls require JavaScript. The theme directory remains available.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** maintain theme palettes and catalogue metadata in data/themes.json.
- **Do** maintain complete world structures in scripts/worlds.mjs, visual rules in assets/css/worlds.css and behaviour in assets/js/worlds.js.
- **Do** preserve theme URLs, résumé download, portrait, full no-JavaScript facts and the themes.html directory.
- **Do** retain keyboard equivalents, visible focus, labelled controls and reduced-motion support.
- **Do** regenerate actual page screenshot previews after visual changes.

### Don't:

- **Don’t** restore the superseded shared opening or force all lower records into one visual template.
- **Don’t** recolor the isolated picker through page CSS.
- **Don’t** represent exploration depth, ability keys or collection progress as real performance metrics.
- **Don’t** send field notes, quest exploration or shell commands to a service.
- **Don’t** claim a user approved a screenshot, comp or qualitative brand metaphor.
